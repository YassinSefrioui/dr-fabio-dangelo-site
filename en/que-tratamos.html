Conditions we treat

Bunions (hallux valgus)

A bunion (hallux valgus) is a progressive deformity in which the big toe gradually shifts toward the smaller toes, causing the joint at the base of the toe to protrude outward. As the deformity progresses, it can become increasingly painful and interfere with daily activities.

Common symptoms include:

  • Pain or tenderness around the big toe joint
  • Swelling, redness, or inflammation
  • Difficulty wearing certain shoes
  • A visible bump on the side of the foot
  • Stiffness or reduced movement of the big toe
  • Corns or calluses caused by overlapping toes

If left untreated, a bunion will:

  • Continue to increase in size
  • Cause chronic pain and inflammation
  • Lead to bursitis (inflammation of the protective bursa over the bunion)
  • Result in crowding or deformity of the other toes, including hammer toes
  • Alter the way you walk, potentially leading to pain in the foot, ankle, knee, hip, or lower back
  • Make finding comfortable footwear increasingly difficult

Early evaluation by a foot and ankle specialist can help determine the most appropriate treatment and may prevent the condition from progressing. Not every bunion requires surgery, but timely assessment allows all treatment options to be considered before the deformity becomes more severe.

Other conditions we treat

Bursitis

Bursitis is the inflammation of a small fluid-filled sac (bursa) that cushions bones, tendons, and soft tissues. Around the big toe, bursitis often develops as a result of a bunion, causing swelling, redness, warmth, and increased pain. Treatment focuses on relieving inflammation and addressing the underlying cause to prevent recurrence.

Tailor's bunion (bunionette)

A prominence on the outside of the foot at the base of the little toe. It can cause pain, swelling, redness, and irritation, particularly when wearing narrow or tight-fitting shoes.

Hammer toes

Deformities in which one or more toes become permanently bent at the middle joint. They may cause pain, corns, calluses, and difficulty fitting into shoes.

Common toe deformities

Toe deformities include conditions such as claw toes, mallet toes, crossover toes, and other alignment problems. These can affect balance, comfort, and walking, and often progress without treatment.

Morton's neuroma

A painful condition caused by irritation or thickening of a nerve between the toes, most commonly between the third and fourth toes. Patients often describe burning pain, tingling, or the sensation of standing on a pebble.

Flat feet (pes planus)

Flat feet occur when the arch of the foot collapses or fails to develop properly. While some people have no symptoms, others experience foot fatigue, pain, ankle instability, and changes in walking mechanics.

Foot & ankle arthritis

Arthritis causes the cartilage within the joints to wear away, leading to pain, stiffness, swelling, and reduced mobility. It can affect the toes, midfoot, or ankle and may significantly impact daily activities.

Plantar fasciitis

One of the most common causes of heel pain. It results from inflammation of the plantar fascia, the thick band of tissue supporting the arch of the foot, often causing sharp pain with the first steps in the morning.

Tendon injuries

The tendons of the foot and ankle play a vital role in movement and stability. Injury or degeneration can lead to pain, weakness, swelling, and difficulty walking or participating in sports.

Sports injuries

Sports-related foot and ankle injuries include ligament sprains, tendon tears, stress fractures, cartilage injuries, and overuse conditions. Early diagnosis and appropriate treatment are essential for a safe return to activity.

Ankle instability

Chronic ankle sprains and ligament injuries that cause the ankle to repeatedly “give way,” resulting in pain, swelling, and reduced confidence during walking or sports.
